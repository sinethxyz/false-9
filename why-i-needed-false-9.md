# Why I needed False 9

I spent a lot of time trying to connect the dots.

I wanted to understand how everything fit together. My interests, the things I learned, the projects I started, the things that failed, the things I became obsessed with and the directions I wanted my life to move in.

More than anything, I wanted to understand where all of it was going.

I wanted the map before I had walked the territory.

But there is a problem.

**The dots do not exist yet.**

You can only connect them backwards.

Looking backwards, a life can appear strangely coherent. One decision led to another. A failure produced a skill. A random interest became important years later. A person you happened to meet changed the direction of everything that followed.

Looking forwards feels nothing like that.

The information is incomplete.

You do not know which project will work.

You do not know which skill will become useful.

You do not know which opportunity will appear.

You do not know which person will matter.

You do not even know who you will become after experiencing things that have not happened yet.

The information required to understand the path is produced by walking the path.

I realised that I had been asking the wrong question.

I kept asking:

**How do I connect the dots looking forward?**

I cannot.

So the question became:

**How do I move intelligently when I cannot see the dots yet?**

That is where False 9 came from.

I stopped trying to predict the entire sequence and started thinking about the field in front of me.

What is true right now?

Where is the pressure?

Where is space opening?

What are the incentives?

Where is attention concentrated?

Where is it missing?

Where would my presence create the greatest favourable change?

Should I move?

Should I stay?

Should someone else take the ball?

I do not need to know the next twenty moves.

I need enough understanding to make the next one.

Then reality changes the field.

So I read it again.

This also changed how I thought about identity.

I spent too much time asking what I was supposed to be.

Engineer?

Designer?

Founder?

Researcher?

Writer?

Strategist?

Those questions assume there is a correct position that I am supposed to discover and permanently occupy.

I no longer think that way.

The objective can remain stable while the position changes.

Sometimes the field requires me to build.

Sometimes it requires me to understand.

Sometimes it requires me to design.

Sometimes it requires me to transfer execution to somebody far better positioned than I am.

Sometimes it requires me to do absolutely nothing.

The point is not to move constantly.

The point is to position correctly.

I still do not know where all of the dots will connect.

That judgement belongs to the future.

For now, I have the field in front of me.

**Looking backwards, connect the dots.**

**Looking forwards, read the field.**

That is why I needed False 9.
